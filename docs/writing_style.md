# Writing Style Guidelines

This document outlines the professional writing style, tone, and structural guidelines for enterprise strategy memos targeting long-running AI agents, autonomous execution loops, and persistent agent architectures.

> **Important Note:** This guide is strictly a stylistic and structural reference. It does NOT contribute factual data, research claims, or operational arguments. All memo content, metrics, and domain-specific frameworks must be derived exclusively from the approved outline (`output/outline.md`) and the synthesized research files (`research/`).

---

## 1. Tone and Voice

Memos must command authority, maintaining an operator-led, analytical, and highly structured tone. Write with the voice of an elite AI systems architect or research engineer who possesses deep technical and strategic expertise.

* **Pragmatic and Operator-Led**: Avoid speculative marketing hype, buzzwords, or generalities. Focus strictly on execution reality, long-horizon state maintenance, context window degradation, background execution constraints, and direct system dynamics.
* **Objective and Fact-Driven**: Ground every claim and argument in concrete benchmarks, architecture metrics, capital expenditure profiles, and real-world system deployments.
* **Decisive and Authoritative**: Use active, assertive language that demonstrates structural understanding of enterprise and AI execution environments. Do not hedge or use weak qualifiers. Address operational dynamics directly and with conviction.
* **Humble but Expert**: Present deep, structural insights without overclaiming success, bragging, or using excessive superlatives. Focus on hard trade-offs and structural constraints that govern long-running agentic systems.

---

## 2. Structural & Analytical Patterns

Premium memos avoid unstructured prose, organizing content into high-density analytical patterns:

* **Concise Upfront Summary**: Every memo must begin with an "Executive Summary" that acts as a standalone synthesis of the core thesis, key findings, and competitive architecture landscape.
* **Structural Division**: Organize chapters logically using numbered main headings (e.g., `## 1. ...`, `## 2. ...`) and precise subheadings.
* **Comparative Matrices**: Use dense, standardized markdown tables to contrast architectures, state management mechanisms, or execution paradigms.
* **Analytical Subheading Flow**: For major arguments, principles, or imperatives, favor subheading-driven structures (e.g., `### Imperative 1: ...`, `### Role 1: ...`) instead of simple bulleted lists.
* **High-Spike Profiles**: When defining roles, architectures, or archetypes, use highly structured "Profile" blocks. Always start with a bolded heading followed by a single-paragraph blockquote outlining the core profile, and follow it with standard paragraphs analyzing the role's mandate and operational trade-offs.

## 3. Exemplary Snippets

Use these snippets to guide your sentence structure, vocabulary, and analytical approach:

### Pragmatic and Fact-Grounded Style
> *"The long-running AI agent landscape in 2026 is governed by a fundamental shift: context window capacity is no longer the primary bottleneck—context precision and state drift over multi-day execution loops are. Model execution is cheap; maintaining deterministic trajectory state across dozens of tool interactions is where enterprise value is won or lost."*

### Analytical Trade-off and Strategic Critique Style
> *"The trade-off is stark: unconstrained autonomous loops offer high task throughput but introduce compound error propagation. Without periodic state checkpointing and human-in-the-loop permission gates, an agent running for hours will exhaust context budgets on circular recovery attempts."*

### Strategic Imperative and Economic Logic Style
> *"A long-running agent architecture that relies on naive transcript replay is economically and latency prohibitive. State compaction, structured knowledge extraction, and persistent background workers are non-negotiable primitives for sustained agent autonomy."*
